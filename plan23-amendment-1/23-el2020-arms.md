## What the data supports

- **With the write contracts, EL2020 gets worse.**
  - The contracts' own effect is A2 − A1 (A2 is A1 plus the four contracts): −6.0 points. That excludes zero with questions resampled, but the interval with runs resampled as well reaches +0.6.
  - Every A2 run is below every A1 run.
  - Against A0 (repairs and contracts together): −9.8 points, excluding zero either way.
  - The state layer falls by 12.7 points against both, an interval with questions resampled only (no run-resampled interval is computed for the state layer).
- **Same-period RAG is about 67%** (three runs within 1 point). Every ProjectMemory arm is 26–36 points behind it, with every interval far from zero. RAG's September figure, rescored, was 72.7%.
- **Where A2 loses** (loss trace, primary series, mean of 3 runs, 133 current rows per run, a row being one value at one checkpoint; `23-loss-trace-results.md`):
  - **Filing:** 61.7 rows are lost here, against 21.7 in A0 and 25.7 in A1:
    - 36.7 (11–21 distinct values) end at `proposed:same_meeting_conflict`: rival values on one key in one meeting leave it in an open conflict, which the autonomous arm never resolves;
    - 15.0 are claims parked by the cycle-2 checks;
    - 5.3 are expired proposals.
  - **State maintenance:** 3.3 rows are lost, against 24.0 and 22.0. Of the rows that reach this step, A2 loses 10%, against 35–37%, so values that are applied are rarely overwritten.
  - **Net effect:** A2 ends with 28.3 rows (9–12 distinct values) stored with the right status, against about 41 rows (22–28 values) in A0 and A1.
    - That accounts for most of A2's current_state drop.
    - The extra filing losses fall heavily on values the other arms store: rows an A0 or A1 run stores correctly are lost at filing in an A2 run about half the time.
- **Why the conflicts are so many.** I read a sample of 16 keys with a same-meeting conflict in A2 r1, rebuilding the positive asserted claims evaluated under the key in that phase (the rival values themselves are not logged):
  - 10 mix values about different matters. For example, `session.duration.decided` holds "30 minutes plus 15 minutes slack…", "around the 23rd" and "stops after 5 minutes".
  - 2 hold two different gold values on one key (F06 and F38 on `person6.multilingual_model.language_count`).
  - 4 hold variants of one matter.

  So the conflicts mostly come from routing, not from speakers disagreeing. In A0 and A1 the same routing shows up in two ways:
  - as `another_value_won` (21.3 / 15.0 rows per run): the rival wins in the same meeting;
  - as `overwritten_by_non_gold_value` (about 20): the rival wins later.

  A2 turns the first mostly into open conflicts (57% of A0's `another_value_won` rows). Of the rows A0 and A1 lose to overwrites, A2 blocks 19–35% in conflicts and stores 33–44% with the right status (pairing every run of one arm with every A2 run).
- **Read with care:**
  - The conflict losses (36.7 rows per run) depend on the autonomous arm having no reviewer: an open conflict is the contract's intended outcome when a human is in the loop.
  - The parked claims (15 rows) do not depend on that: a cycle-2 park is permanent.
  - Where a conflicted key mixes several gold values, a reviewer could keep only one of them.
  - The comparison says what the contracts do without a reviewer. That matches plan 23's open design decision on autonomous-arm conflicts, flagged in `23-el2020-a0-a1.md`.
