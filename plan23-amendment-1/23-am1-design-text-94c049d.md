```latex
\subsection{D1: Separate Source, Evidence, Interpretation, and State}
\label{subsec:decision-boundaries}
```

**D1.p2** (lines 146-153)

```latex
DR1, DR5, DR6, and DR8 jointly make the distinction between a conversational
record and project state a design problem. Three architectures are possible:
answer from raw records; extract structured assertions and immediately use
them as facts; or preserve boundaries between source, admitted evidence,
interpretation, proposed mutation, and reviewed state. The first retains source
context with little curation. The second makes values directly queryable with
fewer stages. Both leave less opportunity to inspect the point at which an
interpretation acquires persistent state consequences.
```

**D1.p3** (lines 155-168)

```latex
ProjectMemory selects the third architecture:
\begin{center}
\small
raw source $\rightarrow$ approved evidence $\rightarrow$ extracted interpretation
\\[3pt]
$\rightarrow$ proposed change $\rightarrow$ reviewed state.
\end{center}
These are epistemic and governance boundaries. A source artifact records what
was received; approved evidence records what was admitted for further use; a
claim records an interpretation; a proposal records a candidate change; a
state fact records the outcome projected by the confirmation workflow.
``Approved evidence'' means admitted by the implemented gate, which may itself
be model-assisted. It does not mean that a person verified the statement or
that its content is true.
```

**D1.p4** (lines 170-176)

```latex
The rationale is to make commitments inspectable before their consequences
persist. Separate records also preserve the evidence needed to challenge an
answer or diagnose where information was lost. The cost is additional storage,
coordination, processing latency, and two forms of unavailability: evidence can
fail admission, and an admitted claim can wait for concept or value review.
A claim that is never extracted cannot be recovered by reviewing its
nonexistent proposal.
```

**D1.p5** (lines 178-185)

```latex
The normal write workflow realises these distinctions, but enforcement is
incomplete across all callable operations, as D6 explains. D1 therefore
responds to all four requirements without proving their full satisfaction.
If it behaves as intended, tentative or rejected interpretations should not
appear as settled answers, and returned facts should carry inspectable
evidence. Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} examines these consequences through
state, rejection, unknown, and evidence questions; the aggregate comparison
cannot attribute an effect to record separation alone.
```

\subsection{D2: Admit Evidence before Durable Interpretation}

**D2.p2** (lines 190-195)

```latex
The DR8 tension is where selectivity should operate. Processing everything
preserves opportunities for capture but exposes all content to interpretation
and review. Filtering only at answer time retains a broad searchable record
and can tailor exclusions to a question, but allows incidental material into
durable interpretations first. Selective admission before extraction places
the decision earlier, at the cost of losses before a claim is ever formed.
```

**D2.p3** (lines 197-203)

```latex
ProjectMemory chooses early admission. Source text is stored and segmented;
a persisted gate decision determines whether an evidence span is created.
Approved redactions are applied before extraction. Ignored spans and sensitive
spans routed for review produce no evidence record through this path.
Extraction enumerates admitted evidence, and ordinary answer citations use
evidence records rather than searching raw artifacts. The gate also supports
DR5: admission and redaction remain inspectable parts of the derivation.
```

**D2.p4** (lines 205-213)

```latex
The implemented threshold is permissive within the admitted set. Policies
named state extraction, candidate extraction, and evidence-only all create
evidence that reaches extraction. Evidence-only is therefore \emph{not} an
enforced prohibition on state extraction. This avoids making retained context
unusable, a problem documented during development, but leaves resolution and
confirmation responsible for preventing inappropriate promotion. Local
transcript windows further bound interpretation: a commitment completed outside
the window can be missed. The normal ingestion path does not invoke the
separate discourse-linking mechanism to repair such omissions.
```

**D2.p5** (lines 215-224)

```latex
The selected balance favours retaining potentially useful project context
while excluding clearly incidental material. It does not settle how selective
admission should be. Raw artifacts remain stored, redaction and classification
are fallible, and evidence access checks do not constitute production
authentication or retention policy. Thus DR8 is only partly addressed, and the
gate supplies no privacy guarantee. The expected consequence is fewer
substantive answers about incidental content without excessive loss of required
state. Noise questions and state recall partially test that balance.
Organisational privacy, sensitive-data retention, and an optimal admission
threshold are not evaluated in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}.
```

**D3.p1** (lines 226-228)

```latex
\subsection{D3: Represent Epistemic Status Explicitly}
\label{subsec:decision-epistemic}
\label{sec:polarity-modality}
```

**D3.p2** (lines 230-238)

```latex
For DR1 and DR4, a simple alternative is to treat every extracted claim as an
asserted fact, perhaps weighted by confidence. This retains mentions but
collapses considering a battery into selecting it. ProjectMemory instead
represents polarity and modality separately from review status and fact
lifecycle. Positive asserted claims are classified as accepted interpretations,
positive non-asserted claims as proposed interpretations, and negative claims
as rejected interpretations. These extraction labels are not acceptance of
state. Extraction confidence likewise describes interpretation, not the
probability that a project commitment is true.
```

**D3.p3** (lines 240-249)

```latex
For scalar concepts, asserted positive claims are eligible to propose
confirmed current values. Where no asserted positive candidate exists, a
proposed, hedged, or hypothetical positive claim can instead propose an
unconfirmed value. Confirmation can accept that proposal while preserving
its unconfirmed epistemic status. Explicit negation does not justify inventing
a replacement. Under current development defaults, an asserted negation of an
alternative can create a proposal for a rejected fact. Negating the current
value additionally passes the resolver's authority and affirmation checks.
With the legacy negation policy, exclusion instead makes the current fact
ambiguous and opens a question for review.
```

**D3.p4** (lines 251-258)

```latex
There is a consequential version boundary for collections. The earlier
mechanism gave proposed and negative material a confirmed membership target
and relied on concept meaning and confirmation to prevent incorrect promotion.
That limitation is evidenced in the earlier code and subsequent test changes;
it does not describe the current branch. Current collection proposals
distinguish asserted positive members, unconfirmed positive members, and
asserted negative members targeting rejected status. Hedged negation is dissent;
rejection of an existing member also requires sufficient authority.
```

**D3.p5** (lines 260-270)

```latex
Member-level status reduces one ambiguity without making collections equivalent
to scalar resolution. An agreeing positive claim adds support to an existing
member without automatically upgrading an unconfirmed member. Membership still
depends on correctly interpreted concept meaning, and heterogeneous content
can enter the same broad collection. Extraction can misclassify modality or
negation in either model. DR1 and DR4 are therefore only partially realised
across the full lifecycle. If the mechanism behaves as intended, answers should
preserve current, proposed, and ruled-out distinctions and abstain when no
current value is established. Status and unknown questions directly target
this behavior; rejection questions and development lifecycle probes cover
only the negative transitions represented in their inputs.
```

**D4.p1** (lines 272-274)

```latex
\subsection{D4: Park Unmatched Claims and Review Concept Expansion}
\label{subsec:decision-schema}
\label{sec:dynamic-concept-construction}
```

**D4.p2** (lines 276-282)

```latex
DR7 makes an unmatched claim a normal case. Discarding it keeps the schema
coherent but loses novel state. Forcing it into the nearest concept preserves
a value at the risk of giving it the wrong meaning. Requiring an externally
authored schema provides stable concepts and clear membership rules, but adds
prior modelling work and violates the study's cold-start condition. Generating
a concept immediately avoids those costs but can make every model-generated
category authoritative.
```

**D4.p3** (lines 284-292)

```latex
ProjectMemory chooses delayed commitment: retain the claim, park it, and
propose a reviewed expansion. Open extraction can leave the concept unassigned.
Matching uses the claim's content and existing concepts' descriptions, aliases,
values, and question examples. An expansion contains a candidate definition
and its triggering evidence. Concept review can accept, edit, reject, or merge
it; acceptance or merging releases waiting claims into ordinary state
resolution. A rejected expansion discards waiting claims from this release
workflow without erasing their source evidence. Concept review and value
confirmation are separate decisions.
```

**D4.p4** (lines 294-299)

```latex
This supports an empty initial registry without treating generated labels as
research outcomes. Parking preserves an interpretation without force-classifying
it, but delays answer availability and can leave useful state permanently
unavailable if its concept is never accepted. Alias-based and optional semantic
matching can reduce duplicate proposals; neither establishes equivalence.
Review may itself merge distinct meanings or fail to recognise synonyms.
```

**D4.p5** (lines 301-309)

```latex
The representation choice within an open schema remains substantial. A broad
feature collection uses fewer concepts and supports direct enumeration, but
places greater weight on coherent membership and member lifecycle semantics.
Fine-grained concepts make conflicts and statuses more local, while increasing
fragmentation, near-duplicates, and the work required to resolve broad questions.
The September defaults favour scalar concept generation and attach family
metadata so related concepts can be aggregated for broad list questions. This
is a preference of the generator, not a universal ban on collections: registry,
review, and projection retain collection support.
```

**D4.p6** (lines 311-321)

```latex
The rationale for the current preference is a more local lifecycle combined
with explicit read-side aggregation. It does not establish that fine-grained
concepts are universally better. Development diagnostics found that changing
registry shape did not automatically remove retrieval misses
(Section~\ref{sec:design-evolution}). The intended consequence is useful state
coverage from an empty registry, assessed by the cold-start protocol in
Section~\ref{subsec:eval-cold-start}. Fragmentation and family quality remain
diagnostic properties; reference key-name agreement is not a correctness
endpoint. Moreover, the gold-informed reviewer can influence which concepts
survive. Cold start must therefore be interpreted together with confirmation
sensitivity, rather than as fully unaided schema discovery.
```

## D5

**D5.p1** (lines 323-325)

```latex
\subsection{D5: Separate Authority from Temporal Eligibility}
\label{subsec:decision-resolution}
\label{sec:state-maintenance}
```

**D5.p2** (lines 327-333)

```latex
DR2 and DR3 require a response to newer evidence contradicting accepted state.
Newest-statement-wins is transparent but treats later mentions as replacements.
Highest-extraction-confidence-wins confuses interpretation confidence with
authority. Highest-authority-wins preserves priority but can privilege stale
evidence. Human-only resolution permits contextual judgement at the cost of
reviewing every contradiction. ProjectMemory selects an authority-gated temporal
policy, leaving unresolved cases reviewable.
```

**D5.p3** (lines 335-342)

```latex
The governing principle is that authority determines eligibility to supersede
and recency orders temporally eligible evidence. In the scalar policy, a
challenger must have at least the current fact's authority and a source time
no earlier than the fact's validity start. Eligible challengers are ranked by
authority, source time, and extraction confidence. Authority thus retains
precedence within the eligible set; recency does not override a higher eligible
authority. The same ranking selects an initial asserted positive candidate.
Different initial values tied on all three dimensions produce a conflict.
```

**D5.p4** (lines 344-350)

```latex
Checking eligibility before ranking matters. Otherwise an older,
higher-authority objection can prevent a newer eligible replacement from being
considered. Lower-authority dissent does not overwrite current state. Ineligible
dissent below the action threshold produces no mutation; other unresolved
dissent produces a conflict. A conflict need not retract the standing value:
the ordinary answer can retain it with a caveat. Keeping uncertainty visible
does not imply abstaining whenever disagreement exists.
```

**D5.p5** (lines 352-362)

```latex
The policy is reproducible but incomplete. Authority is assigned at source
artifact level, not inferred reliably per statement. Two participants in one
meeting therefore normally inherit the same authority. Source time is the
artifact timestamp, falling back to ingestion time; it need not identify when
a reported decision took effect. In addition, the explicit tie-to-conflict
check applies when no current fact exists. The supersession branch selects a
maximum without an equivalent check for tied incompatible challengers, so
conflict handling is not uniformly complete. Negation uses different checks:
its current-value branch lacks the positive-supersession temporal gate, and
an earlier positive claim can block a relevant negation through the affirmation
check.
```

**D5.p6** (lines 364-371)

```latex
These limitations qualify both requirements. The intended consequence is
stable state under casual dissent, replacement by eligible later commitments,
and exposed unresolved conflicts. Checkpoint and change questions partially
evaluate those consequences. The AMI workflow normally assigns the same
discussion authority to every transcript, so it does not substantively test
organisational authority hierarchies. Resolver tests provide diagnostic evidence
for selected authority and time cases; they do not establish that this model
matches real project governance.
```

## D6

**D6.p1** (lines 373-375)

```latex
\subsection{D6: Propose Changes before Separate Confirmation}
\label{subsec:decision-review}
\label{sec:proposals-confirmation}
```

**D6.p2** (lines 377-388)

```latex
DR6 concerns the transition from fallible interpretation to accepted state.
Direct model mutation minimises delay. Confidence-threshold approval retains
automation but uses an admission rule whose reliability must be established.
Manual entry keeps responsibility explicit while moving capture and maintenance
back to the user. ProjectMemory instead routes new values, replacements, and
current-default rejection outcomes through a reviewable proposal and a separate
confirmation operation:
\begin{center}
\small
machine interpretation $\rightarrow$ proposal $\rightarrow$ confirmation
$\rightarrow$ projection.
\end{center}
```

**D6.p3** (lines 390-398)

```latex
Human--AI interaction guidance supports making errors correctable and limiting
system action under uncertainty \citep{amershi2019guidelines}. The
project-specific application is to expose the proposed value, concept, prior
value, target status, evidence, and reason before projection. Confirmation can
accept, reject, edit, defer, or request more evidence. Its review record is
stored separately from the proposal and before the requested projection. An
existing fact can also be placed under review without immediately deleting it.
Review identifies a responsibility and contestability boundary; its occurrence
does not establish truth.
```

**D6.p4** (lines 400-406)

```latex
The costs are intrinsic. Review adds workload and latency, requires reviewers
to be available, and can introduce disagreement or error. Deferral and parking
can make a correctly extracted fact unavailable when needed. An edited
proposal is projected without rerunning authority and temporal resolution,
so the reviewer's correction can bypass parts of that policy. Neither the
review record nor the presence of evidence establishes that the evidence was
actually inspected.
```

**D6.p5** (lines 408-415)

```latex
There is also an enforcement gap. Normal confirmation records review before
application, but the separately exposed apply operation accepts pending,
drafted, deferred, and already accepted proposals without checking for an
accepting review. The legacy exclusion branch also changes a fact to ambiguous
during resolution. The repository therefore supports a controlled workflow
but does not enforce a universal invariant that every state mutation has
passed review. DR6 is partially satisfied; claims about the evaluated workflow
must not be extended to every caller.
```

**D6.p6** (lines 417-424)

```latex
The intended consequence is that changing confirmation policy changes which
interpretations become answerable, making quality and availability trade-offs
observable. Section~\ref{subsec:eval-oracle-confirmation} and the paired-system
protocol define autonomous and privileged-review comparisons and acceptance-policy
sensitivity. These partially evaluate the mediation hypothesis. Review counts,
model calls, and tokens are available as workload diagnostics, but they do not
measure human reading time, reviewer accuracy, acceptable delay, or the cost of
keeping a review process staffed.
```

## D7

Lines 426-473.

**D7.p1** (lines 426-428)

```latex
\subsection{D7: Preserve Supersession and Distinguish the Two Clocks}
\label{subsec:decision-temporal}
\label{sec:state-projection}
```

**D7.p2** (lines 430-437)

```latex
For DR2 and DR3, overwriting a value makes current retrieval simple but loses
its prior validity. Appending every value without status preserves the record
while making each question reconstruct currency. ProjectMemory instead
preserves current and superseded facts, validity bounds, and replacement links.
Temporal database research distinguishes validity in the modelled domain from
the history of its database representation \citep{anselma2026bitemporal}. The
artifact adopts this distinction without implementing a complete bitemporal
query system.
```

**D7.p3** (lines 439-448)

```latex
An accepted scalar replacement closes the prior fact's validity interval,
marks it superseded, creates a successor with a backward supersession link,
and completes the predecessor's forward link. The winning claim's source time
is carried as the proposal's effective time and becomes the new validity start;
the predecessor's validity end uses the same boundary. Creation, update,
review, and event timestamps record transaction or audit chronology. Missing
or unusable effective times fall back to projection time. Source time is thus
a proxy for domain validity, whereas audit timestamps record when the system
processed the change. Distinct fields do not make the proxy an exact effective
date or provide full transaction-time versioning.
```

**D7.p4** (lines 450-456)

```latex
Collections retain independent members instead of superseding siblings.
Rejection also differs from positive replacement. Applying rejection can demote
a matching current row to rejected and close its validity, or record a
ruled-out alternative without displacing another current value. This branch
does not construct the predecessor--successor pair of a scalar replacement.
A rejected proposal, by contrast, records a review decision without creating
a rejected fact about the project.
```

**D7.p5** (lines 458-465)

```latex
Supersession preserves evidence and makes ordinary current-state queries
exclude obsolete scalar values. It costs additional transitions and
consistency obligations. Projection is not enclosed in one transaction:
review, supersession, creation, link completion, proposal status, and event
recording can commit independently. Failure can therefore leave an incomplete
transition. Events are audit records alongside direct fact storage; they are
not the source of truth for replay, and rebuilding records a request rather
than reconstructing state. Auditability is not atomicity or event sourcing.
```

**D7.p6** (lines 467-472)

```latex
The expected consequence is an answerable current replacement with the earlier
fact still identifiable as superseded. Checkpoint recall and change accuracy
partially evaluate this design; the checkpoint protocol also prevents later
inputs from being supplied early. Neither checkpoint answering nor persisted
validity intervals demonstrates arbitrary as-of reconstruction, crash recovery,
or a transaction-time query engine. Those capabilities are not evaluated.
```

**D8.p1** (lines 474-476)

```latex
\subsection{D8: Resolve Maintained State before Loading Evidence}
\label{subsec:decision-retrieval}
\label{sec:question-answering}
```

**D8.p2** (lines 478-485)

```latex
The read-side alternative is to retrieve transcript passages first and ask a
model to infer state for the question. Retrieved passages conditioning
generation are the relevant principle borrowed from RAG
\citep{lewis2020retrieval}. This keeps broad source access and avoids a write-time
schema, but repeatedly places temporal and epistemic interpretation at the
question boundary. ProjectMemory selects a different context-construction order
for DR2, DR4, and DR5: resolve concepts and facts first, then follow evidence
links.
```

**D8.p3** (lines 487-500)

```latex
Concept descriptions, aliases, examples, and observed values associate a
question with generated concepts. Broad list questions may aggregate candidate
concepts and family siblings; specific questions remain narrower. Normal
context contains current confirmed or unconfirmed facts, their evidence,
and relevant conflicts, pending proposals, review sessions, and decision
records. Templates distinguish confirmed from proposed values. If no concept,
current fact, or relevant value answers a specific question, the service can
return an explicit unknown outcome and retain nearby facts only as near
matches. This makes a retrieval gap visible, although unknown alone cannot
distinguish genuine absence from information lost during admission, extraction,
review, or retrieval. Although the public schema defines a disputed outcome,
ambiguous facts are excluded by ordinary current-fact retrieval and normally
reach the unknown branch; a standing confirmed fact can instead carry a
conflict caveat.
```

**D8.p4** (lines 502-511)

```latex
The narrower context is intended to reduce exposure to superseded alternatives
and attach evidence to the selected state. Its cost is restricted answerability.
Ordinary current-state questions do not retrieve superseded facts, rejected
proposals, all negative claims, or events. Current code adds a bounded
exception: rejection-phrased questions can retrieve rejected facts for resolved
concepts, including scalar alternatives never previously current. This extends
the earlier artifact but is not a general search over rejection history.
Negative interpretations must first reach a representable, projected lifecycle
state; rejected expansions and rejected state proposals do not become
answerable merely because they persist.
```

**D8.p5** (lines 513-519)

```latex
Change answering is narrower still. For collection-like or multi-concept
questions, it selects current facts whose earliest linked source artifact is
the most recently ingested artifact. It does not traverse supersession links,
compare arbitrary snapshots, or reconstruct all removals and replacements.
A single scalar change question normally follows the current-value template.
This latest-addition heuristic fits some checkpoint questions but is not a
general temporal-difference engine.
```

**D8.p6** (lines 521-530)

```latex
The expected behavior is current-value accuracy with meaningful abstention
and evidence support, alongside weaker historical coverage.
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} directly targets current and unknown outcomes
and partially tests change and rejection through selected obligations. The
paired comparator is specifically cumulative transcript RAG: ProjectMemory
maintains reviewed state between questions, whereas that baseline reconstructs
answers from retrieved transcript context. This neither characterises every
RAG architecture nor establishes that maintained state is inherently more
accurate. A complete-system difference also cannot isolate retrieval order
from review and write-time work.
```

**D9.p1** (lines 532-533)

```latex
\subsection{D9: Retain Provenance across Consequential Boundaries}
\label{subsec:decision-provenance}
```

**D9.p2** (lines 535-541)

```latex
DR5 requires inspectable evidence, including support for status and replacement.
Attaching only a source title is cheap but cannot locate the supporting
passage. Keeping only the final citation loses admission and interpretation
history. A fully linked derivation graph offers richer reconstruction but
adds modelling and consistency work. ProjectMemory adopts evidence identifiers
and review/event records across principal boundaries, with incomplete
relational linkage between some of them.
```

**D9.p3** (lines 543-550)

```latex
The PROV data model supplies the general distinction between entities,
activities, derivations, and responsible agents \citep{moreau2013provdm}.
ProjectMemory applies traceability through source identity and locators,
admitted evidence and gate decisions, evidence-linked claims, evidence-bearing
proposals, reviews, and facts. Normal projection copies proposal evidence
identifiers to the fact. Application events connect proposals with facts and
record the actor. Citations expose stored evidence text and source identifiers.
This adapts provenance principles; it is not a claim of PROV conformance.
```

**D9.p4** (lines 552-559)

```latex
Three limitations distinguish lineage from evidential correctness. First, a
new fact does not store the winning claim identifier, and proposals lack a
complete claim-to-fact foreign-key chain. Scalar resolution considers all
claims for a concept, while proposal construction copies evidence, authority,
and confidence from the claim that triggered evaluation. If it differs from
the selected winner, the projected value and recorded support can diverge.
Later agreement appends a supporting claim identifier without adding its
evidence identifier to the fact's citation list.
```

**D9.p5** (lines 561-569)

```latex
Second, demoting an existing fact to rejected retains its earlier evidence
rather than copying the rejection proposal's evidence onto it. A citation can
therefore support the former positive value without explaining its rejection.
Recovering who reviewed a change likewise requires review or event records:
the standard apply path does not populate the fact's confirming-actor fields.
Third, public citations expose evidence spans but not stored locators or an
exact mapping from answer sentences to supporting spans. A provenance question
selects an earliest linked artifact using ingestion order, which need not be
the original source of the decisive statement.
```

**D9.p6** (lines 571-580)

```latex
The normal path thus stores and returns linked evidence identifiers when
available, but does not guarantee that every link supports the selected value
and lifecycle status. Claim-level support, state derivation, and auditability
are related but distinct. The intended consequence is an answer whose evidence
permits verification and correction. Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}'s evidence
questions partially test source identification. Its specified claim-level
citation precision and coverage are not yet implemented as the complete
claim-to-span assessment described there: existing scoring primarily checks
meeting availability and judge flags. Full semantic support and complete
lineage therefore remain unevaluated conditions of a stronger provenance claim.
```
